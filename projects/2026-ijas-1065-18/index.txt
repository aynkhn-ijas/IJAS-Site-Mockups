12:T796,Airports are critical gateways of connection, yet they remain some of the world's most energy-intensive facilities. Every day when airplanes take off, release enormous jet vortices powerful enough to even rip apart airplanes behind it. When the amount of energy that is left behind through wind is left to disperse, why can we not harvest it? Therefore, a vertical axis wind turbine should be able to generate electricity from jet wake while simultaneously collecting atmospheric data such as wind speed, temperature and pressure, providing airports with a sustainable and insightful solution. A vertical axis wind turbine was constructed and mounted in an open, unobstructed area to ensure maximum airflow exposure. Instrumentation, including an anemometer, and multimeter, was installed to simultaneously record wind velocity, direction, and energy output. Data collection occurred during three distinct daily intervals (morning, afternoon, and evening) to capture diverse atmospheric conditions. During each session, environmental metrics including air pressure, wind speed, and temperature were recorded every ten minutes alongside energy generation readings, allowing for the calculation of hourly averages to assess the turbine's performance. Data analysis revealed a direct correlation between the simulated wind intensity and electrical output, confirming that small-scale turbines can effectively recover energy from high-velocity air >150+ mph vortices currents. The experiment demonstrated that the windmill successfully converted airflow into measurable voltage stored in the battery pack, validating the concept of wake energy harvesting.
